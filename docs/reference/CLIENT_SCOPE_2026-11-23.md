Proposed Business Management System

Features for a Coconut Product Manufacturing Business

Client Feature Overview  |  23 November 2026

The proposed system will connect purchasing, production, stock, sales and financial records in one place. It is intended to help your team manage the business from receiving ingredients to producing, packaging and selling finished products.

It can support coconut oil, coconut milk, desiccated coconut, food products, cosmetics and other products made using coconut or non coconut ingredients. Each product will use the ingredients, measurements, packaging and quality requirements agreed for your business.

### What the business can manage

| Business area | Proposed coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Stock | Coconuts, other ingredients, bottles, caps, labels, bulk products and finished goods. |
| Production | Recipes, ingredient usage, production batches, packaging, waste and production cost. |
| Sales | Shop billing, wholesale orders, invoices, payments, customer balances and returns. |
| Money | Supplier balances, daily cash, business expenses and financial reports. |
| People and equipment | User responsibilities, with advanced options for staff, payroll and machinery. |

### How the features are grouped

Essential features are the recommended foundation for reliable daily operations. They cover the main controls needed to run this manufacturing and sales business.

Advanced features provide more detailed planning, control and support for growth. Some may also be needed at launch, depending on how your business operates. They can be selected for the first release or introduced in later stages.

This document describes the proposed capabilities. The final features, delivery stages, equipment connections and support arrangements will be agreed before implementation.

### Benefits for owners and managers

See what is in stock, what has been produced, what has been sold and what money is still due.

Reduce repeated record keeping and make it easier to investigate mistakes, stock differences and customer concerns.

## Essential Features for Buying and Stock

These features provide a reliable starting point for managing materials, suppliers and product availability.

### Products ingredients and packaging

- Maintain a clear list of raw materials, other ingredients, packaging materials, bulk products, finished products and byproducts.

- Keep separate records for different product sizes and varieties, such as a 500 ml bottle and a 1 liter bottle of coconut oil.

- Use suitable measurements such as pieces, kilograms, grams, liters, bottles and cartons. Set the correct pack quantities for each product.

- Give products clear names and codes, maintain selling prices and use barcodes where required.

### Purchasing and supplier records

- Keep supplier contact details, purchase prices, agreed payment periods and amounts owed.

- Prepare purchase orders and record what was actually delivered. Handle deliveries that arrive in parts or differ from the order.

- Record supplier invoices and payments separately from deliveries, so materials are not counted twice.

- Record damaged or rejected deliveries, goods returned to suppliers and any supplier credit or replacement agreed.

### Stock visibility and movement

- See quantities at the factory, stores, shop and other agreed storage locations. Distinguish usable stock from damaged, expired or held stock.

- Track ingredients and finished goods by batch, including supplier batch references and expiry dates where required.

- Record stock transferred between the factory and shop, including goods still on the way and partial receipts.

- Set low stock levels and identify products approaching expiry. Give priority to suitable batches that expire sooner.

- Carry out stock counts, compare the count with the system and record approved reasons for shortages, damage or other differences.

### Practical example

When a delivery of coconuts, another ingredient and empty bottles arrives, the team can record the supplier, quantities and relevant batch details. Stock increases when the delivery is accepted, while the supplier invoice and payment remain separately visible.

## Essential Features for Production and Quality

Production records will show what should be used, what was actually used and what the business received from each batch.

### Recipes and production batches

- Maintain an approved recipe or formula for each manufactured product, including coconut and non coconut ingredients.

- Record the intended production quantity and the ingredients and packaging expected for that quantity.

- Keep the recipe used for each batch, so a later recipe change does not change older production records.

- Record the actual ingredients taken from stock, their batches, additional quantities used and unused materials returned.

- Record the finished quantity produced, production dates and new batch numbers. Identify batches that are still being made or are complete.

### Packaging waste and production cost

- Handle bulk production and packaging as separate activities when required. For example, produce bulk coconut oil and then pack it into retail bottles.

- Deduct bottles, caps, labels and other packaging used, and add the finished packs to stock.

- Record useful byproducts and waste separately, including the reason for abnormal loss or damage.

- Compare expected output with actual output and calculate a basic cost from the ingredients and packaging recorded.

### Quality checks expiry and batch tracing

- Record agreed quality checks when materials arrive and when products are made. Keep unsuitable batches on hold until an authorized person decides what to do.

- Record production and expiry dates where needed, and prevent normal use or sale of batches marked as rejected, expired or unsafe.

- Find the ingredient batches used in a finished product batch and the sales or deliveries connected to that batch.

- Record the decision to release, reject, rework or dispose of affected goods. Keep the reason and the responsible person.

### Practical example

For a bottled coconut product, the team can see the recipe used, ingredient batches consumed, bottles and labels used, quantity packed, waste recorded and the quality decision before the products are made available for sale.

## Essential Features for Sales and Payments

The sales features will support both counter sales and wholesale business, with clear records of goods supplied and money received.

### Shop billing and wholesale sales

- Find products by name, code or barcode, enter the quantity and create an invoice or receipt.

- Sell by the relevant unit or weight. Record the accepted weight for weighed products, including the container weight when needed.

- Apply agreed prices, discounts and taxes, with approval for exceptional price changes or discounts.

- Prepare quotations and customer orders, then record deliveries and invoices. Handle orders supplied or invoiced in parts.

- Print or reprint receipts and invoices without creating another sale. Show the original receipt reference.

### Customer payments and credit sales

- Record cash, bank transfers and confirmed card or other supported payments. Allow a bill to be paid using more than one payment method.

- Record part payments and show any balance still due. Keep advance payments available for later use.

- Maintain customer contact details, purchase history, agreed payment dates and outstanding balances.

- Set customer credit limits and place accounts on hold when further credit should not be allowed.

### Returns replacements and refunds

- Find the original sale and record the quantity returned and the reason. Check whether returned goods can be resold, must be held or need disposal.

- Record a replacement, a reduction in the customer bill or an actual money refund as appropriate.

- Require approval for sensitive returns and refunds, and prevent the same original sale from being refunded repeatedly beyond the allowed amount.

### Daily cash control

- Record the cash available when a cashier starts and the cash counted when the shift ends.

- Track cash received, cash refunds, approved cash paid in or out and cash removed for banking.

- Compare expected cash with the actual count and record any approved explanation for a difference.

## Essential Features for Management and Control

Owners and authorized staff will be able to review business activity and check the records behind the totals.

### Expenses and financial records

- Record operating expenses such as transport, electricity, repairs and other business costs.

- Keep customer amounts due and supplier amounts payable, including part payments, advances and credits.

- Maintain linked financial records for purchases, production, sales, payments and stock adjustments.

- Provide income, expense and financial position reports for management and the accountant, based on the transactions recorded.

- Make corrections through a clearly recorded adjustment, credit or reversal, while retaining the original transaction history.

### Daily and monthly reports

- Review sales by day, product, cashier, customer and location, with the ability to inspect the related invoices.

- Review current stock, low stock, stock approaching expiry and differences found during stock counts.

- Review production quantities, ingredient usage, waste and batches awaiting completion or quality approval.

- Review customer balances, supplier balances, expenses, payments and daily cash closing results.

- Compare sales with recorded product costs to understand product margins. More detailed profitability depends on the costs entered and the costing options selected.

### User access approvals and history

- Give owners, managers, cashiers, stores staff and production staff access appropriate to their work.

- Control who may change prices, give larger discounts, release held goods, approve stock adjustments or process refunds.

- Keep a history of important actions and approvals, including who performed them and when.

- Keep your business information separate from other businesses using the platform. Restrict sensitive financial and salary information to authorized users.

### Protecting business records

- Use individual logins and agreed access controls. Set up regular backups and an agreed process for recovering business records.

- Provide staff training for the agreed workflows and confirm the support and recovery arrangements before launch.

## Advanced Features for Production and Purchasing

These options add more detailed control where production, procurement or quality processes require it.

### Production and material planning

- Plan upcoming production using confirmed orders, usable stock, expected deliveries and agreed safety stock.

- Identify ingredients or packaging likely to run short and suggest what to buy, make or transfer.

- Use supplier lead times and minimum order quantities when preparing purchasing suggestions. Keep final purchasing decisions under staff approval.

### Complex production and approved alternatives

- Manage products made in several stages, such as preparing an intermediate product, blending, finishing and packaging.

- Track production steps, work areas or machines, and record progress through the agreed process.

- Allow approved alternative ingredients, with the correct quantities and an approval record. Record rework and partial batch completion.

### Detailed product costing

- Add agreed labor, machine, energy, outsourced processing and overhead costs to production cost.

- Include relevant freight and other purchase related costs when calculating material cost.

- Share production cost across products and useful byproducts using an agreed method, and compare planned cost with actual cost.

### Supplier comparison and outside processing

- Raise internal purchase requests, record supplier quotations and compare price, quantity and delivery terms before placing an order.

- Track company owned materials sent to another business for processing, the products expected back and the processing charge.

- Distinguish your own stock from goods held on behalf of another party when that business arrangement is needed.

### Detailed quality and recall management

- Maintain detailed quality requirements for each product and record measurements, results and inspection approvals.

- Investigate an affected ingredient or product batch, identify related batches and customers, block remaining stock and record recovery or disposal actions.

- Link customer complaints to the relevant product, sale or batch for follow up.

## Advanced Features for Finance People and Equipment

These options extend the system beyond day to day production and billing.

### Banking budgets and foreign currency

- Compare imported bank statement entries with recorded payments and investigate missing or unmatched amounts.

- Set budgets by business area and compare planned spending with actual results.

- Record purchases or sales in agreed foreign currencies, retain the exchange rate used and show exchange differences when payments are settled.

### Staff attendance leave and payroll

- Maintain employee details, agreed pay arrangements, joining dates and assigned work locations.

- Record working time, overtime and leave, with approval where required. Connect production work time to labor cost when selected.

- Prepare salary calculations and payslips using the agreed earnings, deductions and employer contribution rules. Apply separate access for salary information.

### Machinery assets and maintenance

- Keep a register of equipment, purchase details, location and status.

- Record scheduled servicing and repair work, completion details and the related material, labor or service costs.

- Track the reduction in equipment value over time for accounting purposes, using the method agreed with the accountant.

### Customer loyalty and promotions

- Set up loyalty membership and record points earned, used, expired or reversed.

- Offer agreed promotions, quantity discounts and customer or wholesale price lists, with clear start and end dates.

- Review customer purchase patterns and complaints to support service and follow up.

### Specialist stock and management reporting

- Track selected stock items individually by serial number where required.

- Compare results across branches, products, customers and production batches, using the recorded sales, stock and cost information.

- Keep separate records and accounts for additional legal businesses owned by the same group. Group wide financial consolidation can be discussed as a separate requirement.

## Advanced Access and Implementation

The following options and preparation steps should be matched to the way the business will use the system.

### Offline billing when internet is unavailable

- Allow a registered till to continue agreed cash sales using previously downloaded products, prices and an assigned stock allowance.

- Keep those receipts on the till and show which sales are waiting to be sent back. Send them when the connection returns without creating the same sale twice.

- Show any stock or payment differences for a supervisor to resolve. New recalls or rule changes only reach a disconnected till after it reconnects.

- Offline stock, payment methods and permitted working time must be agreed. Card approval, credit sales, returns and loyalty redemption may require an internet connection.

### More locations and equipment connections

- Extend access to additional factories, shops and warehouses, with central visibility and location based staff permissions.

- Connect suitable barcode scanners, receipt printers, cash drawers and weighing scales. Compatibility must be checked against the selected equipment.

- Agree any connection to a payment service, online store, accounting package or other existing system separately, including what information needs to move between them.

### A typical working day

Receive ingredients and packaging, record their batches, prepare a production batch, record actual usage and output, complete quality checks, transfer finished packs to the shop, issue customer bills and close the cashier shift. Management can then review the stock, sales, costs and balances behind that day.

### Preparing for the first release

- Confirm the product list, pack sizes, recipes, quality checks, locations and the staff who will use each part of the system.

- Agree the priority advanced features, payment methods, equipment, report layouts, tax settings and any payroll requirements.

- Prepare starting stock, customer and supplier balances and the existing records to be transferred. Check these figures before live use.

- Test the agreed workflows with real business examples, train the staff and confirm the launch, backup and support arrangements.
